# 08. CGO red-team review of the Sanket outreach plan

Source: Chief Growth Officer colleague in Axon (model: DeepSeek Flash), asked to red-team files 01 to 07 as plain text, no tools. Nothing was executed or sent.

Headline: the four contradictions found are symptoms of one failure. Five workstreams were built in parallel with no single owner and no pre-send sign-off.

## 1. Six biggest weaknesses (most serious first)

1. **Wrong instrument for the goal.** Cold email at 150 prospects is a volume motion. "3 signed design partners" is a trust motion: it needs data access, weekly founder time and a co-build commitment that no SDR sequence can extract. In Indian SME manufacturing this closes by referral, trade association, ERP/Tally partner or a known intermediary. The constraint is credibility, not sends.
2. **Funnel math has no basis and no margin.** 12% positive reply is roughly 4 to 10x realistic cold B2B reply rates to Indian SMEs. Plan for 1 to 3% total reply, under half positive. At 150 sends that is about 2 to 5 positive conversations, not 3 signatures. 5 experiments over 150 prospects is about 30 per arm, too small to call a winner. 2% signed equals the goal exactly: zero buffer.
3. **Pricing leak and premature anchoring.** Growth says no pricing before checkout is verified, but the SDR script quotes Rs 14,995 and the emails imply it. At design-partner stage the figure should not exist externally.
4. **ICP and list disagree.** Hardware ICP is distributors and retail chains, but research returned tool and fastener manufacturers with dealer networks. Half the plan targets an undefined segment, with sizes mostly unverified. If the 30 days fail you cannot tell whether offer, message or list was wrong.
5. **"Signed" is undefined and data readiness is untested.** No document is specified (LOI, paid, free), no partner commitments are listed, and nothing checks whether clean SKU-level demand history exists and can be obtained (Tally, ERP, Excel, Shopify). Risk: 3 hollow signatures that never onboard.
6. **No owner or metric past the reply.** The plan measures inputs (emails sent, experiments run) not outputs (data screens passed, pilot letters out, signed), and stops at signature with no owner for activation or retention.

## 2. Five decisions the founder must make before anything is sent

1. **One vertical or two?** Recommend one: Apparel, for 30 days. Learning only compounds inside one vertical, and a 100/50 split halves the odds in both.
2. **What counts as "signed"?** Recommend a one-page 90-day pilot letter (free or deferred fee, price locked) committing the partner to data access, a weekly 30-minute call and case-study rights.
3. **Mention Rs 14,995 externally?** Recommend no. Delete it from the SDR script and emails today and gate it on billing verification.
4. **Cold SDR volume or founder-led warm?** Recommend founder-led: LinkedIn plus WhatsApp plus two referral paths per target, email as support. Indian SMEs reply to known senders.
5. **What qualifies a lead?** Recommend a hard 4-signal gate: right vertical, verified size, named reachable decision-maker, confirmed SKU-level history. 40 targets max, not 150.

## 3. Cut or simplify

- Cut Hardware for this cycle (all 50 prospects and the conflicting ICP work).
- Cut the 5-experiment programme. One funnel, one dashboard.
- Cut the Day 1/5/12 drip to two touches: one short personal email, one founder LinkedIn/WhatsApp follow-up.
- Cut the 0 to 100 scorecard and 20-minute script down to a 5-question call plus the 4-signal gate.
- Cut price and tier work and generic angles. Replace with 25 first lines built from real signals (new SKU launches, festive-season stockouts, channel expansion).
- Trade 150 sprayed prospects for 40 hand-qualified ones.

## 4. Monday morning checklist (in order)

1. Stop all sends. Pull the email and SDR assets.
2. Pick the single vertical and write it down. Defer the rest.
3. Define "signed" in one sentence: which document, paid or free, what the partner commits to.
4. Verify billing and checkout with a named owner and date. Strip every price mention from script, emails and deck until it passes. If it cannot pass by Wednesday, ship the free 90-day pilot letter with a price-lock clause.
5. Rebuild the list at 40 max, each with verified size, named decision-maker and evidence of SKU data.
6. Rewrite the sequence to two touches. Delete the fake "Re:" subject and the drip.
7. Write the 5-question data screen and make it a gate before any discovery call is booked.
8. For 15 of the 40, find a referral path (investor, association, CA, ERP/Tally partner) and ask for an intro.
9. Stand up one funnel dashboard (sends, replies, positive replies, calls, data screens passed, pilot letters, signed) with an owner and a 5pm daily update.
10. Book the first five founder calls. Send the first ten personalised emails only after steps 1 to 9 are done.

No. 12% positive reply is roughly 4 to 10x what cold B2B email to Indian SMEs delivers at this sender credibility (plan 1 to 3% total reply, under half positive), so 150 cold prospects will not produce the three signatures this goal assumes.
