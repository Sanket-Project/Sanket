# SANKET: 30-day plan to recruit 3 design partners
Author: Growth Marketing Manager (Axon, Marketing Management), model GPT-6.1-Sol. Run date 2026-10-09.
Status: proposed plan, not executed work. SANKET has no customers; outreach must not imply traction, proven accuracy or ROI. Rs 14,995/month is an internal assumption, not validated willingness to pay. Do not mention pricing externally until checkout and paywall behaviour are re-verified.

Goal: 3 signed design-partner agreements by Day 30, each with a named operational owner, an agreed forecasting use case, permission to use suitable data, and a scheduled kickoff; at least 2 reach a first forecast review. A positive reply or booked call is not a recruited partner. A design partner is not automatically a paying customer.

## Week by week
### Week 1: readiness and first 30 contacts
- Growth Marketing Manager: define the design-partner agreement (proposed 4-week evaluation, narrow scope, weekly feedback, clear exit terms; decide internally whether participation is free, do not imply it until approved). Define activation = approved data imported, first forecast generated and reviewed with the partner. One funnel tracker and experiment log; keep Apparel and Hardware separate. Own the pre-flight gates: no first send until all pass.
- Lead Generation: build and validate 150 unique prospect records (proposed 100 Apparel, 50 Hardware, a focus choice not evidence of better conversion). Capture role, industry, source, relevance, contact channel, suppression status. Deduplicate across experiments.
- Email Marketing: adapt the 3-email sequences to an honest design-partner invitation; configure sender authentication, opt-out handling and suppression; launch the first 30 only after approval.
- Copywriter: public one-page site copy and experimental hooks; say what SANKET does, who it is for, what participation involves and what remains unproven. No accuracy guarantees, invented testimonials or unsupported savings.
- SDR: apply the scoring rubric; finalise qualification criteria, discovery script and pilot proposal; offer discovery slots and start the 14-day cadence.
- Deliverables: approved pre-flight checklist, public page, demo/sample forecast, 150-record list, tracker, approved invitation, first 30 contacts.
- Dependency: Product/Engineering must validate product, billing and demo; qualified counsel must review the outreach/data basis. Marketing roles cannot sign these off.

### Week 2: next 60 contacts, qualify demand
- Lead Gen: validate the next 60 records; identify association or ecosystem introduction routes without assuming access.
- Email: contact the next 60 and follow up Week 1 prospects; watch hard bounces, complaints, opt-outs, replies daily.
- Copywriter: seasonal stockout checklist with separate Apparel and Hardware examples, examples clearly labelled illustrative.
- SDR: discovery calls (process, pain, data, decision-maker access, willingness to give feedback); tailored pilot proposal within one business day of a qualified call; disqualify without use case, data access or accountable owner.
- Growth: review early signals and objections; record every targeting or copy change; coordinate approved, securely handled sample forecasts.
- Deliverables: 90 cumulative contacted, discovery notes, experiment observations, first proposals.

### Week 3: final 60 contacts, secure commitments
- Email: contact the final 60 by about Day 16 so each gets its 14-day cadence; stop at once on opt-out.
- SDR: resolve proposal objections, seek signed agreements, confirm scope, data permissions, kickoff date, weekly availability. Target 2 signed by end of week (a target, not a forecast).
- Lead Gen: replace invalid records before contact; report qualification differences between industries.
- Copywriter: FAQs on onboarding, data requirements, forecast limits.
- Growth: coordinate first imports and forecast reviews with Product/Engineering; never mix Apparel and Hardware in one workspace. If fewer than 2 qualified opportunities by Day 18, diagnose offer, targeting and product readiness before increasing volume.
- Deliverables: 150 cumulative contacted, signed agreements where obtained, kickoff plans, documented product/data blockers.

### Week 4: close, activate, capture learning
- SDR: finish cadences, seek the third signed agreement, record why each qualified prospect joined, declined or delayed.
- Growth: aim for 3 signed and at least 2 first-forecast reviews; run feedback sessions; report recruitment and activation separately.
- Email and Copywriter: onboarding messages and weekly feedback prompt; stop acquisition follow-ups for recruited partners.
- Lead Gen: document which segments and sources produced qualified conversations, not just replies.
- Deliverables: partner roster, activation status, experiment log, next-month backlog. If fewer than 3 join, report the actual outcome, do not redefine success.

## Pre-flight checklist (before the first email; each item needs an owner and recorded pass/fail)
1. Sender domain: legitimate, brand-identifiable, monitored reply inbox, volume suited to its history.
2. SPF/DKIM/DMARC configured, tested, aligned.
3. Genuine business postal address in the approved footer.
4. Suppression list: deduplicated, existing opt-outs excluded, opt-outs processed across channels.
5. Billing/paywall: Product/Engineering re-tests checkout success and failure, cancellation/refund flows, invoices, partner access; partners must not be unexpectedly charged or blocked. No external pricing before this passes.
6. Public one-page site live on mobile and desktop: positioning, design-partner terms, contact route, privacy info, no invented social proof.
7. Demo/sample forecast: industry-specific, synthetic or authorised data, assumptions and limits labelled.
8. DPDP/legal review by qualified counsel: lawful basis, source provenance, notices, opt-out, retention, partner data terms. Public contact details are not automatic permission.
If a gate fails, delay the send. Removing pricing from an email is not a substitute for fixing billing and access readiness.

## Five low-cost experiments (150 unique prospects: 40 + 40 + 40 + 20 + 10)
Directional learning tests, not statistically powered. Keep cohorts disjoint; never declare a winner from one or two responses.
1. Pain-led vs design-partner subject line. H: a specific inventory problem earns more relevant replies. 20 vs 20, same body and cadence. Metric: positive replies per contacted prospect (opens not decisive). Stop: after the 14-day window; under 5 positive replies overall means no ranking.
2. LinkedIn-first vs email-first. H: a LinkedIn intro lifts qualified-call conversion enough to justify SDR time. 20 vs 20. Metric: held qualified calls per prospect and SDR minutes per held call. Stop: after 14 days; small differences are inconclusive.
3. Sample forecast vs discovery-call offer. H: a bounded sample reduces perceived risk. 20 vs 20; explain required data and permissions first. Metric: held qualified calls per prospect, analyst time per qualified opportunity. Stop: cap at 5 samples and 60 minutes each; stop earlier if safe data handling is not possible.
4. Seasonal stockout checklist. H: a practical industry checklist starts conversations with operators who ignore a product pitch. 20 prospects, compared descriptively. Metric: qualified conversations per prospect, not downloads. Stop: one asset, one follow-up, 14 days.
5. Partner or association introduction. H: introduced prospects convert better than cold. Seek introductions to 10 distinct prospects through legitimate routes; imply no endorsement. Metric: introductions obtained, held qualified calls, staff time. Stop: cap 4 staff hours; none by Day 14 means report the access constraint.
Shared safety rule: pause a cohort if 2 of its first 20 emails hard-bounce or any spam complaint appears (a conservative rule, not an industry benchmark).

## Funnel math for 150 contacted prospects (GUESSES, not facts)
- Base: 12% positive replies = 18; 67% become held qualified calls = about 12; 25% of those sign = 3 partners (about 8% held-call rate, about 2% signed rate overall).
- Downside: 6% = 9 replies; 56% = about 5 calls; 20% = about 1 partner.
- Upside: 16% = 24 replies; 67% = about 16 calls; 25% = about 4 partners.
The 3-partner goal is plausible but fragile; one lost opportunity changes the outcome. Estimates assume decisions inside 30 days; procurement, data access or product issues may push it later. Track negative replies separately.

## Top six risks and mitigations
1. Billing or paywall failures undermine trust: enforce the gate, verify partner access, keep pricing out of external material until re-verified.
2. Offer too broad across two industries: separate messaging, demos and workspaces; pick a narrow planning use case.
3. Poor or unavailable historical data: qualify data early, agree minimum requirements with Product/Engineering, no accuracy promise before evaluation.
4. Compliance or deliverability trouble: legal review, source records, authenticated sending, suppression, easy opt-out, conservative volume.
5. Interest without activation: require named owner, data plan, kickoff date, weekly feedback commitment; track time to first forecast review.
6. Tiny samples create false confidence: log raw counts, audience differences, effort, downstream outcomes; label results directional or inconclusive.

## Weekly reporting template
Week ending / Owner: Growth Marketing Manager
- Readiness: gates passed/failed; billing and partner-access status; remaining blocker, owner, due date.
- Funnel (Apparel / Hardware / Total): unique contacted; hard bounces; positive replies; negative replies; opt-outs/complaints; calls booked; calls held; qualified opportunities; proposals sent; signed partners; approved data imported; first forecast reviews completed.
- Rates (always numerator and denominator): positive replies / contacted; held qualified calls / contacted; signed / held qualified calls; activated / signed.
- Experiments: hypothesis and cohort; variants and sample sizes; counts and observed difference; effort/cost; downstream effects; confidence (directional / inconclusive); decision (stop / refine / retest); learning.
- Partner feedback: most important problem heard; data or onboarding friction; product limits observed; decline reasons.
- Next week: three priorities with owner and deadline; decisions or dependencies needed; outlook for 3 partners with assumptions.
Day 30: report actual signed and activated counts, evidence behind the next acquisition decision, unresolved blockers. Recruiting 3 partners starts the learning loop; it does not establish product-market fit or paid demand.
