# SANKET: first outbound lead plan
Author: Lead Generation Specialist (Axon, Growth & Outreach), model GPT-6.1-Sol
Run date: 2026-10-09. Status: proposed hypotheses, NOT validated demand. Company examples, contacts and legal requirements were not independently verified.

Goal: recruit design partners and scoped pilots, not sell a proven outcome.

## 1. Ideal customer profile per vertical
Start with India-based companies that have an identifiable planning owner, recurring replenishment decisions and usable SKU-level sales, inventory and stockout history (roughly 12-24 months where available; assess suitability, not a hard rule).

### Apparel and Fashion
- Size/type: 20-250 employees; regional brands, D2C brands with offline retail, multi-store retailers managing size/color variants and seasonal collections.
- Geography: Bengaluru, Delhi NCR, Mumbai, Tiruppur, Ahmedabad.
- Pilot fit: one repeatable category with replenishable products. Avoid starting with exclusively one-off collections.

### Consumer Electronics
- Size/type: 30-300 employees; accessories brands, regional distributors, omnichannel retailers with many SKUs and frequent product transitions.
- Geography: Delhi NCR, Bengaluru, Mumbai, Chennai, Hyderabad.
- Pilot fit: one accessories or established-product category. Avoid new-product forecasting as the first test.

### Pharmaceuticals
- Size/type: 50-500 employees; pharma distributors, healthcare supply businesses or mid-sized manufacturers with a bounded planning use case.
- Geography: Hyderabad, Ahmedabad, Mumbai/Pune, Baddi.
- Pilot fit: clearly approved planning-only scope, preferably de-identified operational data.
- Qualification gate: establish intended use, validation requirements and quality/security review before accepting a pilot. Do not imply SANKET is GxP validated or 21 CFR Part 11 compliant.

### Agro
- Size/type: 20-300 employees; packaged agri-input brands, seed/input distributors, agri-equipment suppliers with seasonal stocking decisions.
- Geography: Maharashtra, Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh, Punjab/Haryana, Telangana/Andhra Pradesh.
- Pilot fit: one region, one seasonal product category. Keep agri inputs separate from fresh produce.

### Hardware
- Size/type: 20-250 employees; tools, fasteners, electrical fittings or building-hardware distributors and regional retail chains.
- Geography: Delhi NCR, Mumbai/Pune, Ahmedabad/Rajkot, Chennai, Bengaluru.
- Pilot fit: one branch or category with repeat orders and a mix of fast and slow-moving SKUs.

Priority: test Apparel and Hardware first, then Consumer Electronics. Pursue Agro when a seasonal planning window is near. Run Pharmaceuticals as a separate, qualification-heavy track. This ordering is a recommendation, not evidence of traction.

## 2. Exact job titles to email, and why
Use as title-search strings; actual titles vary. Contact one primary owner per company first.

| Vertical | Titles | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Apparel | Head of Merchandise Planning; Head of Supply Chain; Founder / COO | category, assortment and inventory planning; replenishment and availability; likely sponsor in small brands |

## 3. Where to find leads (public sources)
Company discovery:
- Apparel: brand websites, store locators, CMAI public directories, apparel trade-event exhibitor lists.
- Electronics: brand/distributor sites, ELCINA public listings, electronics trade-event exhibitor lists.
- Pharma: company sites, Pharmexcil public listings, pharma exhibition directories.
- Agro: agri-input company sites, industry-association listings, agri exhibition directories.
- Hardware: distributor sites, local chamber directories, tools/building-material exhibition lists.

Role and contact discovery:
- Manually review public LinkedIn profiles, leadership pages, job postings, conference speaker pages.
- Job postings that mention demand planning, inventory or replenishment are a signal, not proof of intent.
- Use explicitly published business contact channels. A generic company inbox can be used to ask who owns planning.
- Do not guess email formats, buy lists, bulk-scrape profiles, or treat public visibility as consent.

Verification record per lead: company URL, vertical, role evidence, contact source and date, reason for fit, contact-permission status. A published address is source-verified, not necessarily deliverable or consented. Mark missing info "unverified" and do not hand an unverified personal address to outreach.

First batch: manually qualify 10 companies each in Apparel and Hardware, then expand only after learning which problems and roles produce useful conversations. Association listings and their accessibility were not checked.

## 4. What to say: one angle and subject per vertical
- Apparel: test whether size/color-level probabilistic forecasts and stockout-aware demand correction improve replenishment for one category. Subject: "Design-partner pilot for size/color replenishment"
- Consumer Electronics: test whether forecast ranges help balance stockout risk against excess inventory in one established category. Subject: "Pilot idea for electronics inventory planning"
- Pharmaceuticals: a tightly bounded demand-planning evaluation, intended use and quality requirements first. Subject: "Exploring a scoped pharma demand-planning pilot"
- Agro: region-level seasonal stocking decisions for one input category. Subject: "Design-partner pilot for seasonal agro stocking"
- Hardware: test replenishment recommendations across fast and slow SKUs at one branch. Subject: "Pilot for hardware reorder planning"

Common structure: "We're building SANKET, an industry-specific demand forecasting and replenishment platform, and looking for early design partners. [One relevant, source-backed observation about their business.] Would a pilot focused on [bounded problem] be relevant? We would agree the data scope, baseline and success criteria together. Are you the right person to discuss this?"

Rules: do not claim customers, accuracy gains, cost savings or regulatory compliance. Lead with the planning problem, not foundation-model jargon. If asked about cost, describe Rs 14,995/month as the indicative Scale subscription and confirm pilot pricing separately.

## 5. How to email
Sequence and timing:
- Max three emails over about 14 days: day 1 intro, day 5 follow-up, day 12-14 close-out.
- Send in the recipient's local business hours; treat timing as a test.
- Follow-up 1: add a concrete pilot scope or one qualification question. Follow-up 2: politely close the loop, offer an easy opt-out.
- Stop on an objection, unsubscribe, hard bounce or clear lack of fit. Pause automation when someone replies.

Deliverability:
- SPF, DKIM, DMARC; a clearly branded sender domain, no deceptive lookalikes.
- Start with low, steady volume matched to manual qualification.
- Concise plain text, truthful subjects, minimal links; no first-touch attachments or tracking pixels.
- Monitor bounces, complaints, provider warnings. Keep a suppression list; never re-add opted-out contacts.

Consent and legal guardrails (need current legal review, not verified here):
- India DPDP: do not assume B2B outreach or a public work email is exempt; identifiable work contact details can be personal data and public-data exceptions are narrow. Prefer opt-in intros, event permissions, inbound interest and designated business-enquiry channels. Establish a lawful basis first; an unsubscribe link does not fix a missing basis. Minimize data, record provenance, respect withdrawal, set retention limits.
- CAN-SPAM (US-directed email): accurate headers and subjects, identify as advertising where required, valid physical postal address, clear opt-out honored within 10 business days. B2B is not categorically exempt. It does not replace Indian requirements.

Measure weekly: companies found; qualified (ICP fit, role evidence, contact route); handed to outreach (passed verification and permission checks); outcomes (relevant replies, discovery calls, agreed pilots, pilots started), not raw lead counts.

Before a pilot starts: agree one industry workspace, one category/region, a baseline, evaluation metrics and decision responsibilities.
