# SANKET design-partner outreach playbook (SDR)
Author: Sales Development Representative (Axon, Growth & Outreach), model GPT-6.1-Sol. Run date 2026-10-09.
Purpose: recruit design-partner pilots in India for SANKET (no customers yet). Focus: Apparel and Hardware, 20-250 employees. One industry per workspace. Rs 14,995/month is indicative Scale pricing; pilot terms agreed separately.
This is a proposed operating framework, not executed outreach or a claim about existing product capabilities.

## 1. Lead qualification and scoring rubric (0-100)
Score only against documented evidence. Unknown = "unverified", provisionally 0 (not an automatic disqualifier). Keep an evidence-confidence field so researched prospects are not confused with discovery-qualified ones.

|---|---|---|
| Industry and India fit | 15 | 15 Apparel/Hardware with relevant India operations; 8 adjacent/mixed with a separable qualifying division; 0 unverified or outside campaign |
| Company size | 10 | 10 verified 20-250 employees; 5 credible estimate in range; 0 unknown or outside |
| Inventory problem and impact | 20 | 20 recurring stockouts/excess/planning workload with quantified impact; 10 clear but not quantified; 5 plausible from research; 0 no evidence |
| Data availability and usability | 15 | 15 exportable dated SKU-level sales and inventory plus product/replenishment info; 8 partial with feasible cleanup; 3 exists but access uncertain; 0 unverified/unusable |

| Pilot readiness and ownership | 15 | 15 named champion, agreed window, regular participation; 8 interested but timing/ownership unresolved; 0 no evidence |
| Security and lawful data-sharing fit | 10 | 10 requirements understood and feasible, authorised minimised sharing possible; 5 review pending, no known blocker; 0 unknown or infeasible |
| Commercial path | 5 | 5 indicative Scale price acknowledged, plausible budget/approval path; 3 price acknowledged, budget unresolved; 0 unverified |

Relevant contacts: Apparel: Head of Merchandise Planning, Head of Supply Chain, Founder/COO. Hardware: Inventory Manager, Head of Procurement, COO/MD.

Hard disqualifiers / stop conditions:
- Outside India operations, Apparel/Hardware focus or 20-250 employee range: exclude from this campaign (not unsuitable forever).
- No relevant forecasting or replenishment use case.
- Explicit opt-out, do-not-contact or suppression: stop regardless of score.
- No lawful, permitted route for outreach or data sharing: hold until resolved.
- Requires security controls, certifications or integrations SANKET cannot provide, with no narrower acceptable pilot.
- No usable minimum dataset and no feasible way to get one.
- Requires guaranteed accuracy, guaranteed ROI or fabricated references.
- Requires one mixed-industry workspace and rejects separate scope.

Score bands: 80-100 priority discovery candidate (propose a pilot only after data, security, ownership, success criteria are verified); 65-79 good, resolve specific gaps first; 45-64 research or permission-based nurture; 0-44 deprioritise.
A high score never overrides permission status or suppression. Every active lead has one owner, one next step, one next-step date.

## 3. 14-day multi-touch cadence
| Day | Channel | Action | Goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Email 1 | Approved, personalised plain-text intro: one verified relevance signal, one planning hypothesis, honest design-partner status, invitation to a 20-minute conversation, easy opt-out | Test relevance, find the right owner |
| 4 | LinkedIn | One touch only: a short connection note OR message. Explain the planning problem, do not repeat the email or pitch right after acceptance | Establish context, alternative route |
| 7 | Email 2 | Industry-specific question about process or data; explain narrow scope and agreed baseline; no demonstrated-results claims | Surface pain and pilot feasibility |
| 10 | Phone (optional) | At most one brief call, only if the number is properly sourced and calling is permitted. Ask if it is a suitable time. Skip if permission uncertain | Confirm ownership or arrange discovery |
| 14 | Email 3 | Close the loop politely; offer discovery, a later date, or closure; say it is the final follow-up | Clear next step without pressure |

Limits: max 3 emails and 1 LinkedIn touch; phone is not automatic. Space touches out around holidays. Any substantive reply pauses the sequence. Opt-out: suppress immediately. Hard bounce: stop email, review record. No response by day 14: close the sequence, do not restart or switch address. Track contacted, replied, booked separately; count "booked" only when a meeting is confirmed.

## 4. 20-minute discovery call script
0-2 min, set expectations: "Thanks for making time. SANKET is an early-stage demand forecasting and replenishment product, and we have no customers yet. We're looking for design partners, not claiming proven results. I'd like to understand your process, check whether a narrow evaluation is feasible, and decide together whether there is a sensible next step. Does that work?" Ask: your role in planning, purchasing or inventory decisions? What would make these 20 minutes useful?

2-7 min, current process: how do you decide what to replenish, how much, when? Which decisions happen in Excel, ERP or another system? Who plans, who approves, how often updated? Where does it break down (stockouts, excess, slow movers, supplier delays, manual work)? How do you measure it? What has stopped you solving it?
- Apparel probes: planning style-colour-size, launches, seasons, promotions, markdowns; telling weak demand apart from a missing size.
- Hardware probes: intermittent demand, bulk/project orders, supplier lead times, MOQs, pack sizes; hard-to-plan long-tail items.

7-12 min, data: can you export dated sales or demand at SKU and location level, how much history? Inventory snapshots or movements, stockout records, returns, cancellations? POs, receipts, lead times, MOQs, order multiples? Promotions, prices, attributes, discontinued/new items? Consistent SKU and location IDs? Missing periods? Who can authorise and prepare exports? Can personal data be excluded? Use a de-identified sample; do not demand a full production dataset.

12-16 min, pilot fit: which one category, location or SKU group? Baseline: current plan, a simple method, or both? What decision would a successful pilot help you make? Who owns it internally and attends short weekly reviews? What security or procurement steps? Indicative Scale price is Rs 14,995/month; is that a plausible ongoing budget if the evaluation is useful (pilot terms separate)?

16-19 min, evaluation and guardrails: start with historical backtesting and shadow recommendations, without changing live orders? Which metrics matter (forecast error, bias, planning time, service level, inventory)? How to treat promotions, new products, stockouts, sparse items? What would make either side stop?

19-20 min, recap: "My understanding is: [problem], [current process], [available data], [proposed scope] and [open blockers]. The next step is [action], owned by [person], by [date]. Is that accurate?" Do not push a pilot if the right next step is data review, security review or a polite "not a fit".

## 5. Honest answers to six likely objections
1. "You have no customers yet." Correct. We cannot offer references or proven outcomes. This would be a design-partner evaluation: a limited scope against an agreed baseline with clear exit criteria, not a performance claim.
2. "How will you protect our data?" We should give you a factual description of the controls SANKET actually has before you share data. I won't claim certifications, hosting locations or features not verified. Start with minimised, de-identified exports and agree access, permitted use, retention and deletion in writing. If your required controls are not available, we should not proceed.
3. "We already use Excel or an ERP module." That may be sufficient. First understand what it does well and where planning still needs manual work. A pilot compares against your existing process, not assume replacement. No integration promised until feasibility is confirmed.
4. "Rs 14,995/month is too expensive." That is the indicative Scale price, not an agreed pilot fee. If there is no credible path to value above that cost, we should not recommend buying it. Pilot fees, limits, support and post-pilot terms are agreed in advance; no assumed free pilot or unapproved discount.
5. "How do you measure accuracy?" Agree horizon, SKU/location scope and holdout periods up front; time-based backtesting without leakage; compare against a simple baseline and your current forecast. WAPE and bias for aggregate demand; MAE or MASE for sparse items; MAPE is problematic at zero demand. Stockouts hide demand, so disclose that limit. Lower forecast error alone does not prove better replenishment or ROI.
6. "Why trust a startup?" You should not have to. A limited, reversible evaluation with transparent results builds confidence. Document deliverables, responsibilities, support availability and data-exit arrangements; operational decisions stay with your team. If you require established references we do not yet have, we may not be the right fit today.

## 6. One-page pilot proposal outline
Title: SANKET Design-Partner Pilot, [Apparel OR Hardware], [agreed dates]
A. Objective and scope: planning problem and decision evaluated; one industry per workspace; category, SKU count, locations, horizon, planning frequency; outputs (forecasts, replenishment recommendations, evaluation report, subject to verified capability); exclusions (unconfirmed integrations, automatic ordering, out-of-scope categories); begin in backtesting/shadow mode unless agreed.
B. Data needed: minimum = dated SKU-level sales/demand, product master, available inventory info, replenishment constraints. Helpful = stockouts, returns, POs, receipts, lead times, prices, promotions. Prefer 12-24 months. Agree formats, identifiers, refresh frequency, data-quality owners. Exclude unnecessary personal data; document authorised sharing and handling.
C. Baseline and evaluation: current method plus a simple baseline; fixed time-based holdouts or rolling backtests, same horizon and scope; no future-data leakage; document exclusions and missing-data handling; note sales may understate demand during stockouts.
D. Success metrics: primary = agreed forecast-error measure and bias; operational = planning time, recommendation usability; optional live outcomes (service level, stockouts, excess, turns) only if duration supports it. Thresholds agreed after initial data review and before evaluation; no invented improvement percentages; define inconclusive-result conditions.
E. Timeline: week 1 data/security review, feasibility gate, baseline and metrics; week 2 historical evaluation and error review; weeks 3-5 shadow recommendations and weekly feedback; week 6 final assessment and continue/extend/stop decision. Six weeks may not show live inventory impact.
F. Commitments: SANKET delivers the evaluation and explains methods and limits, provides named ownership and support, follows documented data terms, is explicit about unavailable capabilities. Partner nominates champion and decision-maker, provides authorised exports and business rules, reviews outputs, keeps purchasing decisions.
G. Commercial and exit: pilot fee, taxes, limits, payment terms; Rs 14,995/month recorded as indicative Scale pricing; no automatic conversion or renewal; termination, data return/deletion, output ownership, separate permission for any name, logo or case study; dated decision meeting and a named next-step owner.
